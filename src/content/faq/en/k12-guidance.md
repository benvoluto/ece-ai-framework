You can borrow from it. You cannot rely on it, because two of the seven
principles on this site exist in no other AI framework anywhere — and they are
missing from every one of them for the same reason. Every framework in
circulation was written either for K-12 or for generic nonprofits.

**There is no developmental-appropriateness test in any of them.** A tool that
is unremarkable for a fifteen-year-old can hit the mechanism of development
directly for a two-year-old. K-12 guidance has no place to ask that question.

**A four-year-old cannot notice an AI system, object to it, recognize it as
non-human, or report harm from it,** and leaves no record of dissent. That is
qualitatively different from K-12, where student voice is at least available in
principle. A framework that assumes the person affected can raise a hand has
assumed away the entire population this site is about.

The law is different too. Most early childhood settings are not schools, so most
of what K-12 guidance says about FERPA and student-privacy statutes does not
reach them. See [What the law says](/law).
