"Back office is safe" is the one shortcut this framework must not take, because
in early childhood the back office is where the child data lives.

Subsidy billing contains family income, immigration-adjacent documentation and
custody information — the most sensitive material in the building, and none of it
is in a classroom. Grant narratives routinely contain child vignettes. Enrollment
records, licensing documentation and CACFP reconciliation all name individual
children and families.

So the tiers here are not drawn between "administrative" and "classroom." Tier 1
is defined by the *absence* of child and family data: marketing copy, menus,
newsletters naming no child, professional development, general grant boilerplate,
staff scheduling against ratio requirements. The moment a child or a family is
named, you are in Tier 2, which means a written agreement, a register entry, no
consumer chatbots, and retention and deletion terms.

That is a real distinction and an easy one to apply. It is also the distinction
most policies get wrong. See [Start here](/start).
