Nobody can tell you, and anyone who gives you a number should be asked where it
came from. There is no survey of AI use among Head Start grantees outside
districts, private centers, or family child care homes. The RAND figures people
quote cover public-school pre-K only. That is a gap we state rather than fill.

But the question behind the question is usually "do I need to deal with this
yet?" — and there the answer is clearer. AI is not mostly arriving as a purchase
decision. It is arriving as a product update.

Your observation and documentation app adds a feature that drafts learning
stories from classroom photos, and it is on by default. Your Head Start data
system ships an agentic feature that drafts family engagement notes and flags
children for follow-up. Nobody in the building chose an AI tool, nobody signed
anything, and a compliance system nobody thinks of as an AI tool is now flagging
individual children.

That is the most likely real-world path by which AI enters an early childhood
program. The practical consequence: treat a vendor feature release as an
adoption event. Register it, tier it, and require a human determination before
any flag informs a referral or a service decision. See the [cases](/cases).
