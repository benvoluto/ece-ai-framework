This one is flat. No AI tool drafts a report, smooths a narrative, summarizes a
teacher's notes into one, or flags which incidents ought to become one. The
report is written by the person who saw what they saw, in their own words, and
nothing sits between the observation and the page.

What makes this line unusual is that no authority anywhere addresses it. There is
no state child welfare agency guidance on it, nothing in CAPTA guidance, and
nothing from any early childhood body. The unresolved questions underneath are
real ones. When does reasonable suspicion attach if a tool flags first — at the
flag, or at the moment the adult forms a view? Is an AI-smoothed narrative still
the reporter's own contemporaneous observation, which is the thing the record is
supposed to preserve? Nobody has answered these.

Because nobody has answered them, a flat prohibition is the defensible position.
It is simple to state, simple to audit, and it costs providers nothing: no
program's reporting duty becomes harder to discharge because a person wrote the
report. This is a place where the field can be first rather than late.
