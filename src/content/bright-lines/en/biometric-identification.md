The common versions of this are mundane: a check-in and check-out system that
recognizes a face at the door, or a photo app that automatically tags which child
is in which picture. Both create a biometric template of a child, and both are
usually adopted for convenience rather than for any safety requirement the
program could articulate.

The legal position flips the risk map most people assume. The Illinois Biometric
Information Privacy Act (BIPA) applies to "private entities" and expressly
excludes state and local government agencies. A private center or a nonprofit
grantee therefore carries more exposure here than a public school district does —
with a private right of action and liquidated damages behind it. The amended
Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) Rule expressly includes
biometric identifiers, including voiceprints and facial templates, within
"personal information," so the federal consent machinery attaches as well.

There is an honest difficulty underneath this line, and the framework states it
rather than papering over it. Consent has to come from a parent on behalf of a
child who cannot give it. That is a live legal question with no clean answer, and
it is the reason the consent required here must be express, separate from the
enrollment paperwork, and revocable — a family must be able to say no, or to
change its mind later, without that affecting the child's place in the program.
