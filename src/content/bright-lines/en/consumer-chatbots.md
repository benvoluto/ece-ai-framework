rule: Do not put child or family personal information into a consumer chatbot with no written agreement.
shortRule: No child or family information into a free or consumer AI chatbot without a written agreement behind it.

This is the line most often crossed by accident, and usually by someone trying to
do a good job quickly. A teacher pastes a set of observation notes into a public
chatbot to turn them into a parent-friendly summary. A director pastes a family's
situation in to get help drafting a difficult letter. Neither of them thinks of
it as sending a child's record to a company, because it does not feel like
sending anything anywhere.

Under section 1303.22, a Head Start grantee that pastes child observations into a
consumer tool has arguably made an undisclosed third-party disclosure. There is
no consent from the parent, no agreement anyone could review, and no entry in the
section 1303.24 disclosure log. Each of those is a separate gap, and together
they describe a disclosure the program cannot account for to the family whose
child it concerns.

Here is the part providers most often miss: the violation is complete whether or
not anything leaks. The question is not whether the company misused the text or
suffered a breach. The question is whether the program can say where a child's
record went and produce the agreement that governs it. A free consumer tool with
nothing signed behind it fails that question at the moment the paste happens.

The remedy is not to ban AI writing help. It is to route that work through a tool
the program has an agreement with, entered in the register, so the same task
stops being a disclosure nobody can account for.
