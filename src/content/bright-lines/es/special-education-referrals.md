A referral is a consequential decision about a named child, and it carries the
strongest human-decision requirement anywhere in this framework. A tool may
surface a pattern, sort a caseload, or remind a teacher that a checklist is
overdue. It may not be the reason a child is referred. The evidence behind the
referral has to be evidence a person has examined, and the determination has to
be one that person is prepared to sign.

The failure mode is not hypothetical. It is fully documented in adjacent
child-serving systems that adopted screening algorithms earlier than early
childhood did. Oregon discontinued its child-welfare screening tool after it
flagged a disproportionate number of Black children. The Allegheny Family
Screening Tool drew a civil-rights inquiry from the Department of Justice over
disability bias. Both were built by serious people with good intentions, and both
were reviewed before they were fielded.

The practical test for a director is short. If the tool went away tomorrow, could
you still explain, from the record, why this child was referred? If the answer is
"the system flagged her," the referral was not independently determined and this
line has been crossed.
