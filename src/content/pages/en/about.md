This site began as a build spec: a working document that set out, section by
section, what a field-wide framework on the use of AI in early childhood
education would have to contain to be useful to the person it is aimed at. That
person is a director of a single center, or a family child care provider,
reading between nap time and pickup, with no lawyer, no IT department and about
twenty minutes.

The spec was written to be built from, not to be read. This site is what it looks
like once it is built: the principles, the bright lines, the tiering, the
regulatory crosswalk, the vendor questions and the artifacts turned into pages
someone can actually read, print and sign.

That makes it a complete draft of an argument, with its sources shown. Here is
what it does not make it.

- **It is not legal advice.** It is not a substitute for a lawyer who knows your
  state, and on the questions where your state is what matters, it says so.
- **It has not been reviewed by counsel.** Not one page. The regulatory
  crosswalk is the part of this site most likely to be relied on and most likely
  to be wrong in a particular state, and it is openly waiting on a legal review
  that has not happened.
- **It has not been adopted by anybody.** No association, agency, funder or
  standards body has endorsed it, reviewed it, or agreed to any part of it. The
  principles have not been ratified, because there is no one to ratify them.
- **There is no body behind it.** No chair, no members, no funders, no advisers,
  no legal partner. Not withheld — they do not exist. Where a framework of this
  kind would carry names, this site leaves the space visibly empty. Inventing
  names would be the first dishonest thing on a site whose whole argument is
  about disclosure.
- **It has not been re-checked for currency.** Several claims rest on sources
  with a short shelf life. The list of those is on this page, and none of them
  has been verified against a primary source by the people who built the site.

Read it the way you would read a careful draft from a colleague who has done the
reading: worth the twenty minutes, worth arguing with, and not yet worth citing
as authority.
