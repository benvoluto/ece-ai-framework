## 1. For an existing family handbook

Add as a new section, usually after the section on records and privacy.

### Artificial intelligence (AI) in our program

[PROGRAM NAME] uses some software that is described as artificial intelligence,
or AI. We use it to do paperwork faster so that the adults here spend more time
with children.

We use these tools to help draft routine writing such as newsletters, menus and
reminders, to translate routine messages into the languages our families speak,
and for office work such as billing, enrollment and licensing records. A staff
member reads and corrects anything a tool drafts before it reaches a family.

We do not let a tool decide anything about a child. No AI tool decides
enrollment, removal from the program, discipline, or a referral for special
education or early intervention. A person makes those decisions, on evidence a
person has reviewed. We do not use tools that read children's faces, voices or
bodies to guess how they feel or how engaged they are. We do not use AI in any
report of suspected abuse or neglect. We do not use machine translation for
screening results, meetings about services, conversations about a child's
safety, or decisions about whether a child qualifies for a program; for those we
use a qualified bilingual staff member or interpreter. We do not enter a child's
name or information into a free public chatbot.

Some uses of AI require your written permission first, on a separate form. Those
are photographs and video of your child, recordings of your child's voice, any
tool that scans a face or voice to identify your child, and any company's use of
your child's images, voice, work or records to develop or train its AI. You may
refuse any of these, and you may withdraw permission later, without any effect
on your child's care or enrollment.

We keep a written list of every AI tool we use, what it is used for, and the
agreement we hold with the company that provides it. You may ask to see that
list, and any agreement on it, at any time. Ask [NAME], [ROLE], at [PHONE] or
[EMAIL].

## 2. For an existing staff handbook

Add as a new section, usually inside the section on confidentiality or
technology use.

### Acceptable use of AI tools

Employees of [PROGRAM NAME] may use an AI tool that appears on the program's
Approved Tools Register, for the purpose listed there and for no other purpose.
The register is kept at [WHERE THE REGISTER IS KEPT]. A tool not on the register
is not approved, including a free tool and including one an employee already
uses personally. Adding a tool requires approval in advance from [NAME OR ROLE].

An approved tool may be used for writing that names no child and discloses
nothing about a child — newsletters, menus, flyers, postings, an employee's own
lesson planning. It may be used for records work such as billing, enrollment,
licensing and food program records only where the register lists that tool for
that purpose. The employee reviews and corrects every piece of AI-drafted text
before it is sent, and remains responsible for its accuracy.

The following are prohibited without exception, and a violation is grounds for
discipline under this handbook:

1. Entering a child's or family's name, image, voice, record or identifying
   detail into any tool that is not on the register.
2. Using AI at any point in documenting or reporting suspected child abuse or
   neglect.
3. Using machine translation in a screening-result conversation, an IFSP or IEP
   meeting, a child-protection conversation, or an eligibility determination.
4. Acting on a tool's statement about an individual child before a qualified
   person has reviewed the underlying evidence and made the determination.
5. Using AI as a basis for enrolling, refusing, disciplining, excluding or
   disenrolling a child, or for initiating a referral for special education or
   early intervention.
6. Using any tool that infers emotion, affect or engagement from a child's face,
   voice or body.
7. Using any tool that identifies a child by face or voice, or uploading a
   child's images, video, voice, or work to any tool, unless the family's
   separate written consent is on file and [NAME OR ROLE] has confirmed it.

An employee who is unsure asks [NAME OR ROLE] before using the tool; asking is
never a violation. An employee who learns that child or family information has
gone into an unapproved tool reports it to [NAME OR ROLE] the same day.
Reporting promptly and in good faith is not itself a disciplinary matter.

## 3. For an existing photo-release form

This block matters most. A general photo release does not carry permission for a
company to train AI on a child's images, voice or work. The amended Children's
Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) Rule — effective June 2025, full
compliance April 2026 — requires separate verifiable parental consent before a
child's personal information is disclosed to a third party for AI development,
and the Federal Trade Commission treats such a disclosure as not integral to the
service and so outside a general consent. The amended Rule also treats biometric
identifiers, including voiceprints and facial templates, as personal
information. So the release must say what it does not cover, and the training
permission must be asked for on its own.

**3a. Add this paragraph to your existing release, directly above the signature
line.**

This release covers photographs, video and audio recordings of my child made by
[PROGRAM NAME] and used by [PROGRAM NAME] for the purposes described above. It
does not cover, and does not give permission for: any use of my child's images,
voice, work or records by a software company or other outside company to
develop, train, improve or evaluate artificial intelligence; or any tool that
identifies my child by face or voice, including automatic photo tagging and
photo or voice sign-in. [PROGRAM NAME] will ask for those separately, in
writing, and I may refuse without any effect on my child's care or enrollment.

**3b. Add this block below the existing signature line, as its own consent with
its own signature.**

### Separate consent: AI training and face or voice identification

Answer each line. You may leave this section blank, and your child's care and
enrollment will not change. You may withdraw either permission at any time by
telling [NAME] in writing; we will stop within [30 DAYS] and ask the company to
delete what it holds.

**What "training" means.** Companies build AI by feeding it large numbers of
real examples. If a company trains on your child's photos, video, voice or work,
those examples become part of how its software behaves for everyone, and they
usually cannot be pulled back out later. That is different from a company simply
storing your child's photo so you can see it.
