## How to use this document

**This policy is built to be cut down.** The eight sections below are
independent. Nothing in Section 5 depends on Section 3 still being here, and no
section tells you to "see Section 6." Read the headings, keep the sections that
describe work your program actually does, delete the rest, replace every
[SQUARE BRACKET], then sign and date the last page.

Two sections earn their place in every version. Section 2 is the never-list —
it is what makes the rest of the policy defensible to an owner, a board or a
licensor. Section 8 is the review date — without one, an AI policy is stale
within a year and nobody notices.

Paragraphs marked **Multi-site only** apply to organizations running more than
one site or holding delegate agreements. Single-site programs delete them.

Throughout this policy, **AI tool** means any software that generates text,
images, audio or recommendations, or that classifies, scores, transcribes or
analyzes what a person wrote, said, or did. That includes an AI feature added
to a tool the program already uses.

## Section 1 — Governance and approval

1. No AI tool is used for the work of [PROGRAM NAME] until it has been approved
   under this section and entered in the Approved Tools Register.

2. **An AI feature switched on inside a tool we already use is a new adoption.**
   A default-on feature in an existing product requires approval at its tier
   before anyone uses it, the same as a new purchase does. The most common way
   programs adopt AI without deciding to is by not reading a release note.

3. Approval authority:

   - **Tier 1** (no child or family data): approved by [NAME], [ROLE].
   - **Tier 2** (touches child or family data): approved by [NAME], [ROLE], who
     confirms a written agreement is on file before approval.
   - **Tier 3** (about an individual child, or used by a child): approved in
     writing by [NAME], [ROLE] together with [NAME], [ROLE], with the vendor
     answers and the family consent language in front of them.
   - **Tier 0** (the eight never-uses in Section 2): not approvable by anyone
     at [PROGRAM NAME], at any level.

4. **Multi-site only.** Site directors approve Tier 1 for their own site without
   asking anyone. [ROLE] approves Tier 2 for all sites. Tier 3 is approved in
   writing by [ROLE] and [ROLE] and applies organization-wide; no site adopts a
   Tier 2 or Tier 3 tool that is not already on the organization's register.
   Delegated authority is deliberate: if every request queues behind one person,
   staff stop asking and start using tools quietly.

5. Anyone at [PROGRAM NAME] may propose a tool. Proposals go to [NAME], [ROLE],
   who answers within [10 BUSINESS DAYS] — yes, no, or what is still needed.

6. A tool is approved for a **stated purpose**, not in general. Using an
   approved tool for a different purpose at a higher tier requires new approval.

7. Approval is recorded with a **review date** no more than [12 MONTHS] out.

## Section 2 — What [PROGRAM NAME] never does

These eight uses are not adopted at [PROGRAM NAME] regardless of who asks, what
it costs, or how well it works. Each carries the reason it is defensible.

1. **No emotion, affect, or engagement recognition on children.** No tool that
   reads a child's face, voice, or body to infer how they feel or how engaged
   they are. The European Union Artificial Intelligence Act, Article 5(1)(f),
   prohibits emotion inference in education institutions outright, and it
   reaches placing on the market and putting into service, not only end use.
   Halberstadt et al. (2020) found prospective teachers 1.36 times more likely
   to show racialized anger bias toward Black children's faces; human raters
   carry that bias, training labels carry it, and models learn it.

2. **No AI output as the basis for expelling, suspending, disenrolling, or
   denying enrollment to a child.** 45 CFR 1302.17 prohibits expulsion for
   behavior in Head Start, and 45 CFR 1302.33 prohibits using screening or
   assessment to exclude children from enrollment or to rank, compare, or
   evaluate individual children for rewards or sanctions. [PROGRAM NAME]
   applies that standard to every child it serves.

3. **No AI output as the basis for a special education or early intervention
   referral** without an independent human determination on the underlying
   evidence. Oregon discontinued its child-welfare screening tool after it
   flagged a disproportionate number of Black children, and the Allegheny
   Family Screening Tool drew a United States Department of Justice
   civil-rights inquiry over disability bias.

4. **No AI anywhere in mandated-reporter documentation** — not drafting it, not
   summarizing it, not flagging it. No authority anywhere addresses this: no
   state child welfare agency guidance, no guidance under the Child Abuse
   Prevention and Treatment Act, and nothing from any early childhood body.
   [PROGRAM NAME] draws the line itself because the questions underneath it —
   when reasonable suspicion attaches, whether an AI-smoothed narrative is
   still the reporter's own contemporaneous observation — have no answer yet.

5. **No child or family personal information into a consumer chatbot with no
   written agreement behind it.** Where Head Start applies, pasting a child
   observation into a consumer tool is arguably an undisclosed third-party
   disclosure under 45 CFR 1303.22, with no consent, no reviewable agreement,
   and no entry in the 45 CFR 1303.24 disclosure log. The violation is complete
   whether or not anything leaks.

6. **No machine translation in Individualized Family Service Plan (IFSP) or
   Individualized Education Program (IEP) meetings, screening-result
   conversations, child-protection conversations, or eligibility
   determinations.** 45 CFR 1302.33 already requires qualified bilingual staff
   or interpreters for these conversations, and the National Center for State
   Courts cautions explicitly against machine translation in high-stakes
   proceedings. Routine operational translation — newsletters, reminders, menus
   — is permitted and encouraged under Section 3.

7. **No face or voice biometric identification of children** — check-in and
   check-out, photo auto-tagging — without express, separate, revocable
   parental consent. The Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA)
   applies to private entities and expressly excludes state and local
   government agencies, so a private center or a nonprofit grantee carries more
   exposure here than a public school district does. The amended Children's
   Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) Rule expressly includes biometric
   identifiers, including voiceprints and facial templates, in "personal
   information."

8. **No vendor training of models on children's images, voices, work, or
   records without separate verifiable parental consent.** The COPPA Final
   Rule, effective June 2025 with full compliance April 2026, requires separate
   verifiable parental consent for disclosing children's personal information
   to third parties for artificial intelligence development, and the Federal
   Trade Commission treats such disclosures as not integral to a service, so
   they cannot ride on a general consent signed at enrollment.

## Section 3 — Data handling by tier

Every tool is placed in a tier before it is approved. The tier is set by what
the tool sees and what it decides, not by which department bought it.

**Read this before you tier anything.** Tier 1 is defined by the **absence of
child and family data**, not by the word "administrative." In early childhood
the back office is where the child data lives. Subsidy billing contains family
income, immigration-adjacent documentation and custody information. Grant
narratives routinely contain child vignettes. Work like that is Tier 2, wherever
it sits on the org chart and whoever does it.

### Tier 1 — Back office, no child or family data

Marketing copy, menus, newsletters naming no child, professional development,
general grant boilerplate, staff scheduling against ratio requirements.

[PROGRAM NAME] requires:

- The staff acceptable-use rules in Section 5 apply.
- A named person reviews the output before anything is sent, posted or filed.
- Family-facing text that AI drafted carries a line saying so.

### Tier 2 — Operations touching child or family data

Subsidy billing, enrollment records, licensing documentation, Child and Adult
Care Food Program (CACFP) reconciliation, grant narratives containing child
vignettes, family communication and translation.

[PROGRAM NAME] requires:

- A **written agreement** with the vendor, on file before first use.
- An entry in the Approved Tools Register.
- **No consumer chatbots.** No free or personal-account tool touches this work.
- Written retention and deletion terms, including how long the vendor keeps the
  data and what deletion actually reaches.
- **Head Start programs:** an entry in the disclosure log under 45 CFR 1303.24.

### Tier 3 — About an individual child, or used by a child

Screening and assessment support, observation narratives, photo and video
portfolios, behavior tracking, referral routing, classroom cameras, anything a
child interacts with.

[PROGRAM NAME] requires everything in Tier 2, plus:

- Full vendor due diligence, using the program's vendor question sheet, with
  the answers and the date written down.
- **Separate parental consent** — specific to this use, revocable, and not
  bundled into the enrollment packet.
- Performance evidence disaggregated for dual language learners (DLLs).
- A **documented human determination** for anything the tool touches that
  affects a child: the person's name, what evidence they looked at, and what
  they decided.
- An **exit plan written before adoption**: how the program gets its records
  out, what the vendor deletes, and how long that takes.

### Tier 0 — Do not adopt

The eight uses in Section 2. [PROGRAM NAME] screens for these first. If a tool
lands here, no further review happens.

## Section 4 — Approved tools

1. [PROGRAM NAME] keeps one **Approved Tools Register**, held by [NAME], [ROLE],
   at [LOCATION OR FILE PATH].

2. Each entry records: tool, purpose, tier, who approved it, approval date,
   whether a written agreement is on file, what data it touches, and the review
   date.

3. **If it is not on the register, it is not approved.** That applies to free
   tools, to trials, to a tool one person uses on one task, and to an AI
   feature inside a product the program already pays for.

4. **Multi-site only.** One register covers the organization, with per-site
   adoption status on each entry. Delegates and individual sites do not keep
   separate registers.

5. Families may ask to see the register at any time, and may ask to see any
   written agreement behind an entry.

6. A tool removed from the register is removed from use the same day, and the
   steps in Section 7 for discontinuation apply.

## Section 5 — Staff obligations

Everyone working at [PROGRAM NAME], including substitutes, floaters,
contractors and volunteers, follows these rules.

**Green — do these freely, on program accounts, with a human reading the output
before it goes out:**

- Draft or rewrite a newsletter, a flyer, a menu, or a policy paragraph that
  names no child.
- Translate routine operational communication — reminders, closures, menus,
  event notices.
- Summarize a public document, a regulation, or training material.
- Draft a job posting, an interview question set, or a professional development
  plan.
- Build a schedule against ratio requirements.
- Rewrite something you already wrote in plainer language.

**Red — never, on any account, without exception:**

- A child's name, date of birth, address, photograph, voice, or record in any
  tool that is not on the Approved Tools Register.
- Any child or family information in a free or consumer chatbot.
- Anything on the never-list in Section 2.
- Anything about enrollment, discipline, exclusion, or a referral.
- Any mandated-reporter documentation.
- Machine translation in an IFSP or IEP meeting, a screening-result
  conversation, a child-protection conversation, or an eligibility decision.
- Turning on a new AI feature in a tool you use, before it is approved.
- Program work in a personal AI account.

**When you are not sure:** do not put it in — ask [NAME], [ROLE] first, and
asking is never the wrong call.

Staff also: tell families when a family-facing document was AI-drafted, report
anything a tool did that looked wrong under Section 7, and read and sign the
staff acceptable-use one-pager at hire and at each annual review.

## Section 6 — Notice to families

1. [PROGRAM NAME] gives every family written notice of what AI the program
   uses, in what part of the work, and what information it touches. Notice goes
   out at enrollment and again whenever the program adopts a new Tier 2 or Tier
   3 tool, at least [14 DAYS] before that tool is used.

2. **Notice is not consent, and this program keeps them separate.** Families are
   told what is in use as a matter of course. Consent is a separate, signed,
   revocable document, and it is required for biometric capture and for any
   vendor use of a child's images, voice, work, or records in model
   development.

3. Consent for those uses is never bundled into the enrollment packet, never a
   condition of care, and may be withdrawn at any time in writing without
   affecting a child's place or services.

4. Families may, at any time: see the Approved Tools Register, see any written
   agreement the program holds with a vendor, ask what information about their
   child a given tool receives, and ask for a human review of any decision.

5. Family-facing notices are provided in [LANGUAGES]. A family may decline a
   Tier 3 use as it applies to their own child, and [PROGRAM NAME] will record
   that and work around it.

6. Questions go to [NAME], [ROLE], at [PHONE] or [EMAIL].

## Section 7 — Incident response

An **incident** is any of these: a tool exposed child or family information it
should not have; a vendor disclosed a breach; a tool turned out to do something
the program was not told about, including training on program data; a tool
produced an output about a child that was wrong in a way that mattered; or
someone used a tool in a way Section 2 or Section 5 forbids.

When one happens:

1. **Stop.** Whoever notices stops using the tool for that purpose immediately
   and tells [NAME], [ROLE], within [24 HOURS]. Reporting an incident you caused
   yourself carries no discipline at [PROGRAM NAME]; concealing one does.

2. **Write it down the same day.** Date, tool, what happened, which children or
   families are involved, what information was touched, and who knows.

3. **Contain it.** Suspend the tool, revoke access, and put the affected work
   back on a manual process while the review runs.

4. **Ask the vendor in writing**, within [3 BUSINESS DAYS]: what data was
   affected, when, who else received it, what has been deleted, and what the
   vendor is doing. Keep the answer and the date.

5. **Tell affected families in writing** within [7 DAYS], in plain language and
   in the family's language: what happened, what information about their child
   was involved, what the program has done, and what they can do. Do not wait
   for the vendor's full account to send the first notice.

6. **Notify whoever your program must notify** — [LICENSING AGENCY], [FUNDER],
   [HEAD START REGIONAL OFFICE], [INSURER] — on the timelines those bodies set.
   The clocks in this section are [PROGRAM NAME]'s own commitments and do not
   replace a reporting deadline set by anyone else.

7. **Decide whether the tool comes back.** [NAME], [ROLE] decides, in writing,
   at the tier the tool sits in under Section 1.

8. **If the tool is discontinued:** request deletion in writing, including
   derived material such as embeddings, model artifacts, backups and vendor
   analytics; ask for written confirmation with a date; export the program's
   records first; remove the entry from the Approved Tools Register; and tell
   staff the same day so nobody keeps using it.

9. Keep every incident record for [7 YEARS] with the program's other compliance
   files.

## Section 8 — Annual review and adoption

[PROGRAM NAME] reviews this policy every [12 MONTHS]. The review covers, at
minimum: every tool on the register and whether it is still in use; every
approval past its review date; incidents since the last review and what changed
because of them; new AI features turned on by vendors during the year; whether
the family notice still describes what the program actually does; and whether
anything in Section 2 needs to be stated more clearly.

**Multi-site only.** [ROLE] reports to the board or owner every quarter on tools
in use by tier, incidents, what changed, and what is pending approval. In Head
Start programs this belongs in existing Policy Council and governing body
reporting rather than in a new standing item.

This policy is adopted by [PROGRAM NAME] on the date signed below. It replaces
any earlier AI guidance the program has issued.
