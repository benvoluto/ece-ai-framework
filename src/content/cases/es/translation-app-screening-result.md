Nothing about this provider's conduct is careless. She has a screening result
she is worried about, a family she wants to talk to today, and no interpreter.
The phone is what she has. This is the most sympathetic scenario in the
library, and the guidance still has to hold.

It has to hold because machine translation fails precisely where this
conversation sits. It is reliable enough for routine operational messages —
newsletters, reminders, menus, closure notices — and genuinely useful there. It
degrades on high-stakes content, where a hedge, a conditional or a clinical
term carries the meaning, with documented safety consequences in medicine and
explicit cautions from the National Center for State Courts. And it degrades
most on the languages with the least training data. Mixteco is not Spanish.
The family with the fewest alternatives is the family the tool serves worst,
which is why this cannot be a rule that bends when an interpreter is
inconvenient.

For Head Start programs this is already a requirement rather than a
recommendation: §1302.33 requires qualified bilingual staff or an interpreter
for exactly these conversations. The framework generalises it as bright line 6,
which covers screening-result conversations, meetings about an Individualized
Family Service Plan (IFSP) or an Individualized Education Program (IEP),
child-protection conversations and eligibility determinations.

What to do on the day:

- **Use a live interpreter for the conversation itself** — your state's
  interpreter line, or the language access resources your child care resource
  and referral agency (CCR&R) can reach. Ask for Mixteco specifically, and say
  which variant if the family has told you.
- **Use the translation app for the message that schedules it.** "I would like
  to talk with you about Ana's screening. I am arranging someone to interpret.
  Is Thursday at 4 good?" is exactly the kind of routine message machine
  translation handles well.
- **Say nothing substantive through the app in the meantime.** Waiting two days
  for an interpreter is better than a result the family half-understands, and
  much better than a result they understand wrongly.

**What this page still owes you.** "Use an interpreter" without a phone number
is not guidance. This case is not finished until the site carries the actual
interpreter line and language access contact for each state, and it does not
carry them yet — the state layer is planned for the next phase of this site,
starting with six priority states. Until then, the number your resource and
referral agency already has is the fastest route.
