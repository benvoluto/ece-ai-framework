question: "Is any information we enter, or that your product collects about children, used to train or improve your models — or anyone else's? If yes, under what consent?"
whyItMatters: "This is the question that decides whether a child's observations, photographs, voice and work become permanent training data. The amended COPPA Rule requires separate verifiable parental consent before children's personal information is disclosed to a third party for AI development, and the Federal Trade Commission has said such a disclosure is not \"integral\" to a service, so it cannot ride on the general consent a family signed at enrollment. The Rule says less about a company training its own models on data it already holds, which is why you ask the question in two halves and write the answer into the contract."

A real answer separates the two cases and names them: no customer content is used
to train our models, no customer content is shared with any other company for model
development, and here is the contract clause that says so. A real answer will also
tell you what changes if you turn a particular feature on.

A dodge answers only the second half. "We never sell your data" and "we do not share
data with third parties" leave the company's own internal training untouched — which
is exactly the gap in the federal rule. So does "data is used only to improve the
service," which is a description of training, not a denial of it.

Record the answer, the name and role of the person who gave it, and the date. If the
answer is yes under any conditions, ask which consent form covers it and get a copy.
