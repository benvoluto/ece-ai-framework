The first is a wrong output about a named child — a garbled observation, a mistranslated note to a family, a summary that attributes something to the wrong child — and the person who has to fix it is the director, today. The second is a failure at the vendor: a breach, an outage, a model change that alters what the product says. Both end up at the same desk, because in a single-site program the reader of this page is the whole organization."

A real answer gives a route and a clock: a named channel for a wrong output about a
child, a target response time, and a written commitment to notify you within a stated
number of days of a breach or a material change to how the product works. A vendor
that tells you when it retrains the model is telling you something useful.

A dodge routes you to general support with no timeframe, or defines notification as
an entry in a changelog you are expected to read. "We will notify affected customers
as required by law" tells you the floor, not the commitment.

Write the phone number or address on the sheet next to the date. The point of the
question is that on the day it happens you should not be looking for it.
