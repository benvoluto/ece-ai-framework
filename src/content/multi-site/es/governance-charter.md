A charter is not a policy. A policy says what is allowed; a charter says who
decides, and that is the question a multi-site organization actually has to
answer.

Delegate the decision down to the lowest level that can carry it:

- **Site directors approve Tier 1 without asking anyone.** Back-office uses that
  touch no child or family data should not travel to the central office. If they
  do, nothing else in this structure will hold.
- **A named role approves Tier 2.** One role, named in the charter, for anything
  that touches child or family data. Not a committee, and not "the central
  office" — a role with a person in it and a stated turnaround time.
- **A small group approves Tier 3 in writing, with the crosswalk in front of
  them.** Anything about an individual child, or used by a child, gets a written
  decision from a standing group that has read the regulatory crosswalk for the
  setting in question. Written, because this is the decision someone will be
  asked to justify later.

The reason to delegate is not efficiency, though it is more efficient. It is
that without delegation everything queues behind one person, and staff route
around the queue. That is precisely how the unmanaged yes starts: not with
defiance, but with a site director who needed an answer in March and was still
waiting in June.

Set a turnaround commitment for each tier and publish it. An approval path with
no clock on it is a queue, and a queue is a workaround waiting to happen.
