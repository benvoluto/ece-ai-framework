The same tool presents completely different risks depending on who is holding
it.

A teacher's exposure runs through observation and documentation: what goes into
a note about a child, whose name appears in a prompt, and whether a generated
summary starts to stand in for what she actually saw. A family services worker's
exposure runs through the most sensitive records the program holds and through
conversations where a machine translation is not an interpreter. A billing
clerk's exposure runs through volume and through data that is financial and
identifying rather than developmental. One training deck addressed to all three
will be too abstract to change what any of them does on a Tuesday.

Large organizations can afford to train them differently. Small ones cannot —
a single-site director is running one training for everyone because there is one
of her and one staff meeting. **This is one of the few places where scale is a
genuine advantage rather than a complication**, and it is worth spending the
advantage rather than defaulting to the universal version.

Keep one universal layer: the bright lines, and what to do when unsure. Then
split by role for everything else, and make the role-specific half concrete
enough to be about the work rather than about AI.
