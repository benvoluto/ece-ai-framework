For a Head Start grantee that is a community action agency, a nonprofit, a tribe
or a private entity, FERPA is not the governing law. 45 CFR Part 1303 Subpart C
is, by default.

Subpart C is not thin. It covers consent, the exceptions that permit disclosure
without it, the parent's right to inspect and to seek amendment of records, the
parent's right to review written agreements with third parties, disclosure
logging, and destruction. Read against a vendor contract, the right to review
written third-party agreements is the provision that does the most work: it
means a parent can ask to see what you signed.

**The exception inside the exception.** 45 CFR §1303.21(b) displaces Subpart C
with IDEA Part B and Part C confidentiality for any child who has been referred
to, or found eligible under, IDEA — the Individuals with Disabilities Education
Act — and it does so in any grantee, not only in the district-operated ones.

That means a single grantee can be running two confidentiality regimes at the
same time, child by child, decided by whether a referral has been made. A policy
that names only Subpart C will be wrong for part of the enrollment.
