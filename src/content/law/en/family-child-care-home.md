A family child care home stands where a center stands, with one difference that
changes the practical picture entirely: there is effectively no institutional
privacy floor at all.

The same four sources apply — COPPA on the vendor, state biometric law, state
consumer privacy law, licensing regulations — but none of them supplies what a
district's records office supplies: a standing set of rules about who may see a
child's information, written down before anyone asks.

That is not a reason to write a longer policy. It is a reason to write a short
one and actually sign it. In this setting the document you give families is the
privacy floor, so the useful move is one page that says what tools you use, what
you never use them for, and what happens to a child's information — not a
forty-page framework that cites statutes which do not apply to you.
