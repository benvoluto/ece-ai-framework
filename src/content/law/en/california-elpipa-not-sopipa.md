If you are in California and someone hands you a vendor agreement that recites
SOPIPA, or a policy template built on AB 1584, you are looking at the wrong
statute for your setting.

AB 2799 (2016) did not amend SOPIPA. It added a separate statute at California
Business and Professions Code §22586 — the Early Learning Personal Information
Protection Act, ELPIPA — covering operators of services used and marketed
primarily for preschool and pre-kindergarten purposes. Biometric information is
expressly included in what the statute treats as "covered information."

The practical consequence is precise: an early childhood vendor in California
may be regulated by ELPIPA and not by SOPIPA. A contract that cites only SOPIPA
is describing obligations the operator may not be under, and is silent about the
ones it is.

**What to do with this.** When a vendor sends you a California privacy rider,
check whether ELPIPA is named anywhere in it. If it is not, ask the vendor in
writing which of the two statutes it considers itself subject to for your
program, and keep the answer. That single question separates a vendor that has
looked at early childhood from one that has copied its K-12 paperwork across —
and it is worth knowing which one you are buying from before you sign.

Note that ELPIPA, like SOPIPA and like COPPA, regulates the operator. It is
another reason the leverage in this field sits in the contract.
